## Key Insights Summary

1. **Google AI Mode adoption is still small** — ~1% of US desktop searches, comparable to News/Shopping tabs. Growth has plateaued. Traditional search still dominates.

2. **Ranking paradigm is dead** — "An arbitrary keyword search repeated daily is no longer a great proxy for how well you perform." Shift from rankings to visibility in relevant contexts.

3. **Think in passages, not pages** — "Google is now scrapbooking for us — taking snippets from things and putting them together." SEOs must stop optimizing at the URL level and think at the paragraph/passage level.

4. **Originality is the missing piece** — Risk of "query fan-out = lots of FAQs in short chunks" being misunderstood. The real question: how do we signal that we have the ORIGINAL take, not a summary of a summary?

5. **Quality over volume** — "We should think more about quality rather than volume as a whole, and think more strategically about what type of content is actually really needed."

6. **New KPIs for AI era** (from Dwayne Forester's piece):
   - Chunk retrieval frequency
   - AI citation count
   - Vector index presence
   - LLM answer coverage
   - Semantic density score
   - Attribution rate in AI outputs

7. **Personalization is now real** — AI search results are personalized. SEOs need to go deeper into customer personas and specific use cases — not just broad keyword targeting.

## Key Quotes

Chris Green on the paradigm shift: "A lot of what the industry has pivoted to over the last two years around what good or quality looks like — a lot of that is still close enough that it doesn't represent a complete change of what we actually do."

Aleyda on content strategy: "Content that helps your audience AND drives business goals — the intersection of this is what allows you to create information that actually makes sense for you."

Chris on passages: "We have to stop thinking of the page as the document and think of the parts of the different journeys. That's a big mindset shift for most people."

Aleyda on tracking: "We still need more insights, more tools, better features — but a lot of it is already here in maybe a raw way. This is the start of the new conversation."

Chris on originality: "How do we signify that we have the original take and we're not a summary of a summary? We're still missing that a little bit."
